# Roland Liu

I build small tools to close the gap between "this is annoying" and "this is handled." Most days that means full-stack development and applied AI; some days it means teaching my computer to do something my phone was doing badly — I once wrote a script just so my laptop and phone could share the same message box, because I couldn't justify losing three seconds a day to something a few lines of code could fix permanently.

I'm studying Electrical Engineering at the University of Waterloo, and I spend most of my time at the intersection of software engineering and machine learning — the parts of a system people actually touch, and the parts underneath that quietly decide whether it holds up.

There's a version of engineering that's mostly about novelty, and a version that's mostly about care — getting the boring parts right so the interesting parts can be trusted. I'm more interested in the second kind. A tool is a small argument about what deserves your attention and what doesn't; I like making that argument well.

## Some things I've built

### [Enterprise Regulation RAG](https://github.com/liuroland55/enterprise-regulation-rag)
A full-stack application for querying company regulations and getting grounded, cited answers instead of confident guesses. A self-correcting retrieval pipeline (retrieve → generate → self-grade → rewrite) orchestrated with LangGraph, a FastAPI backend, a Tauri/React desktop client, and both cloud and fully offline model support — because not every organization can afford to send its documents to someone else's server.

### Video Text Extractor
A browser extension for pulling clean, correctly formatted text out of a paused video frame — math notation, code, chemical formulas included. The interesting part isn't any single model; it's the plumbing: routing each frame to whichever OCR service actually handles that kind of content well, and falling back gracefully when one is unavailable or over quota.

### Crosshair
A Windows overlay tool, built the way most of my personal projects start: something I wanted for myself, that turned out to be worth sharing. Configurable, lightweight, and — appropriately for a tool about precision — fussy about pixels.
